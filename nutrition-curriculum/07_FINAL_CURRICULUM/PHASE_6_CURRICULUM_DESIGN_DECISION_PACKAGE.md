## 1. An Important Scope Distinction (stated once, applies throughout)

Phase 4 and Phase 5's findings are about **what this application's own decision-engine needs to
represent** (Phase 4's depth tiers) and **whether the 7-book corpus's content is adequate for specific
questions** (Phase 5's findings) — an application-centric and evidence-centric lens, respectively. Phase
6's question — **how should a human-facing nutrition curriculum be sequenced and scoped** — is a
different lens entirely. A topic Phase 4 tiered `NOT-REQUIRED-FOR-APPLICATION` (e.g., most of the `MET`
biochemistry spine) is not thereby unimportant for a *curriculum* — `KNOWLEDGE_DECISION_DEPTH_MAP.md`
said so explicitly at the time. This document is careful to cite Phase 4/5 findings only where they
speak to a genuinely curriculum-relevant question (mainly: content redundancy/depth questions Phase 2
left unverified) — not to import application-centric tiers as if they were curriculum-design inputs.

## 3. `PHASE_2_HUMAN_REVIEW.md`'s 12 Items — Cross-Referenced, Still Unresolved

Every item below is restated from `PHASE_2_HUMAN_REVIEW.md` (not modified there), with whatever Phase
3/4/5 has since added. **None is resolved by this document.**

### Item 1 — AS3/ACSM/SN4 Architecture (posture: uniform spine / uniform 3-pass / topic-by-topic)

**Phase 2 status:** `SPORT_NUTRITION_ARCHITECTURE.md` found Option C (topic-by-topic role assignment)
best-supported by TOC evidence, but flagged two specific things as **unverified without reading actual
chapter content**: (a) whether AS3/ACSM's TOC-thinner chapters are really thinner or just less
granularly indexed (Option A's stated risk); (b) whether AS3/ACSM/SN4's near-identical-looking hydration
chapters are genuine reinforcement or restatement (Option B's stated risk, citing `TOPIC_OVERLAPS.md`
#6).

**What Phase 5 added:** Register §3.6 directly read AS3's Chapter 4 ("Ergogenic Aids") and ACSM's
supplement chapter (for the `SPORT-05` topic specifically) and found them **genuinely complementary in
approach**, not restatement — AS3 opens with a regulatory/conceptual framework (DSHEA 1994, ergogenic-aid
taxonomy), ACSM teaches via mechanistic case studies (e.g. the beet-juice/mouthwash interaction), SN4 is
the broad ~35-compound reference catalog. **This directly resolves concern (a) above for the supplements
topic specifically** — the TOC-thinner AS3/ACSM chapters are pedagogically distinct, not merely
under-indexed restatements of each other.

**What remains unverified:** concern (b) — the hydration-chapter overlap question — was **not** checked
in either Phase 5 session. This is a genuinely open sub-question, narrower than it was before (one of
two stated risks is now resolved), but not closed.

**Still open:** which posture to adopt (uniform spine, uniform three-pass, or topic-by-topic per
`SPORT_NUTRITION_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s own working table) — a human curriculum-design decision, now made
with more evidence but not answered by it.

## 5. Consolidated Gate 4 Decision Package

**What genuinely blocks Phase 6 from producing a final curriculum architecture** (items requiring a
human/architectural decision, in the order they'd naturally need deciding):

1. **Curriculum spine selection (item 6, A/B/C/D)** — the foundational choice; nearly every other item
   below is easier to decide once this is settled (items 3, 4, 9 explicitly depend on it or interact
   with it).
2. **Clinical scope / Layer 5 depth (item 7, tied to `DEC-099`)** — explicitly not to be resolved by
   Phase 6 per instruction; carried forward exactly as unresolved.
3. **`CANDIDATE_EXCLUSIONS.md` formal ratification (item 11)** — can be decided somewhat independently
   of the spine choice, though the spine may affect *how* core/elective status gets implemented
   (e.g., Option D's act-three applied branches vs. Option C's interleaved units).
4. **Research domain placement (item 9, A/B/C/D)** and **AS3/ACSM/SN4 posture (item 1)** — both now
   better-informed than at Phase 2 (item 1 especially, per §3), still open.
5. **Remaining smaller items (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 12)** — lower-stakes, can plausibly be decided alongside or
   after the above, several explicitly coupled to item 6's outcome.

**What does NOT block Phase 6 from producing preparatory documentation** (already done in this
document, autonomously, per instruction): the cross-referencing in §2–§3 above, and confirming that no
Phase 3/4/5 finding silently closes any of these items.

**Recommendation (not a decision):** if a single question must be asked first, it is **item 6** — the
spine selection — since it reframes or couples to five of the other eleven items. Items 7 and 11 are the
next most consequential (both bear directly on how large the CLIN and elective content blocks are).
