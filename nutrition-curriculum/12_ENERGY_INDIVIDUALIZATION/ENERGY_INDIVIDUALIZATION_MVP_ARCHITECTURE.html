Energy Individualization — Phase 1 MVP Architecture

Individualization from a richer profile, with weigh-in and intake logging demoted to an optional second phase. Companion to ENERGY_INDIVIDUALIZATION_RESEARCH_SPEC.md, …_ARCHITECTURE.md and …_CALCULATIONS.md.

Status: proposal for human ratification, not a ratified design. Scope is a recommendation; every item marked RATIFY is an open decision reserved for a human. No DEC ID is created or amended, no readiness word changes, and no coefficient is invented. Several inputs proposed in §4 have no coverage anywhere in the corpus — they are new capture surfaces that need a source before any number attaches to them.

1 · The shape of Phase 1

Logging is optional, so Phase 1 has to be complete and honest on its own. The honest consequence is worth stating plainly: without observation you cannot verify an estimate, so the interval never narrows. What a richer profile buys is not confirmation — it is better resolution and, more importantly here, less upward bias (§3). The optional Phase 2 is the only thing that can actually close the loop, and it stays strictly additive: a user who never weighs in still gets a complete product, just a permanently wider one.

Phase 1 — required, no logging

  • Enriched one-time profile — work, habitual movement, training, sleep (§4).
  • Decomposed activity estimate — baseline PAL and declared training counted separately, so training is counted once.
  • Banded output — a range with an honest width, never a bare point.
  • Conservative anchoring — the fix for the estimate reading high (§3).
  • One-shot plausibility check — self-reported 3-month weight direction; no logging required.
  • Conservative prescription — deficit taken from the low edge of the band.

Phase 2 — optional, opt-in

  • Weigh-in series and intake completeness — only if the user opts in.
  • EWMA trend + noise-floor check.
  • Sufficiency gate, then intake-balance back-calculation.
  • Hard switchover — replaces the band's value, no blending function invented.
  • Never a precondition. Nothing in Phase 1 may degrade, nag, or withhold a target because this is off.
  • Still excluded: wearable expenditure feeds, Kalman/EKF estimators, independent macro loops, retrospective re-estimation.

3 · Why the estimate reads high — and the four corrections

Your test observation is reproducible from the corpus itself. The shipped PAL points sit at or above the top of the project's own drafted bands — and in the sedentary case, entirely above it.

Illustrative magnitude (arithmetic on the values above, not a new project value): at a resting energy of 1600 kcal/day, a sedentary user gets 1600 × 1.40 = 2240 kcal/day today, against 1600 × 1.25 = 2000 kcal/day at the drafted band's midpoint — roughly +240 kcal/day, about +12%, before any of the other three mechanisms below.

Four mechanisms, all established

  • Points above their band. The table above. Largest and most easily fixed.
  • Double-counted training. The category describes a whole day, but users self-select it by how often they train — so training is counted in the category and again in any later exercise allowance.
  • Self-report inflation. Self-report over-estimates activity while MET tables under-estimate the cost of what was done; the literature describes these as compounding, not cancelling.
  • Band collapsed to a point. Collapsing a range always discards the conservative edge, and the app has already done this twice (activity, and the fluid figure).

Four corrections — each needs sign-off

  • Re-anchor to the drafted bands, so no category ships a point above its own band.
  • Emit a band, not a point, all the way to the UI.
  • Separate baseline PAL from declared training, so training is added exactly once and is visible as its own line.
  • Take deficits from the low edge of the maintenance band, so an over-estimated maintenance cannot silently become an over-generous target.

One caution on the decomposition itself: asking more questions improves resolution and auditability — a user can correct the one component that is wrong instead of a vague category — but it does not automatically improve accuracy. The same compounding self-report and MET-table errors apply to each component, so a decomposed estimate must still be reported as a band. A more detailed number that is presented more confidently would be a net loss.

C5Conservative prescription Ratify — edge policy
loss:      target = M_low − deficit          ← low edge, not the midpoint
gain:      target = M_low + surplus          ← accepts slower gain over accidental overfeeding
maintain:  target = M_low … M_high           ← presented as the band
floor:     target = max(target, MIN_CALORIES)
Inputs
Maintenance band (C3), goal
Output
Energy prescription, tagged with the edge policy that produced it
Why
Directly addresses the high readings: if maintenance is over-estimated, a deficit measured from the midpoint inherits the whole error. Measuring from the low edge spends the band's width on caution
Ratify
The edge policy per goal direction — the loss case is the safety-relevant one; gain and maintain are conventions, not safety choices, and could reasonably differ
Unchanged
The deficit and surplus magnitudes themselves, and every macro formula downstream

Appendix · Abbreviations, symbols and terms

Everything shorthand that appears in this document, in the three companion documents, or in the cited sources.

Energy and physiology

BMR
Basal metabolic rate — energy at complete rest. The field name in the shipped code (bmrKcal)
REE
Resting energy expenditure — what C1 estimates. Measured under less strict conditions than BMR; the two are used interchangeably here
RMR
Resting metabolic rate — the same quantity as REE; the term the cited source titles use
TEE
Total energy expenditure — everything burned in a day. REE × PAL
TDEE
Total daily energy expenditure — the same quantity as TEE; the name consumer products use
EER
Estimated energy requirement — the output of the NASEM framework (C3)
PAL
Physical activity level — the multiplier that turns REE into TEE, i.e. TEE ÷ REE. The source of the high readings (§3)
MET
Metabolic equivalent of task — activity cost as a multiple of a conventional resting rate. The 1-MET convention over-states real RMR by 10–30%
NEAT
Non-exercise activity thermogenesis — movement that is not deliberate exercise: work, commute, chores, fidgeting. What the §4 fields try to resolve
EI / EE
Energy intake / energy expenditure (C8)
ES
Body energy stores; ΔES is their change across a window (C8)
FM / FFM
Fat mass / fat-free mass — the two compartments a dynamic model partitions weight change across
BMI
Body mass index — context only in this app, never a calorie input
LEA
Low energy availability — intake minus exercise expenditure, relative to fat-free mass
REDs
Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport — the consensus framework around LEA. Out of scope for this MVP
CAT2
The clinical assessment tool, version 2, used for REDs screening

Measurement and statistics

SD
Standard deviation — spread within a sample. Used for the weigh-in noise floor
SE
Standard error — the uncertainty of an estimated quantity, e.g. of a fitted trend slope (C6, C8)
SEPV
Standard error of predicted value — how far a prediction equation typically misses for one individual. Published per equation: 342 kcal/d for men ≥19y, 241 kcal/d for women. This document's candidate width for the prior (C3). Not the same as SEE (standard error of the estimate), which these documents do not use anywhere
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error — reported above 30% for wearable expenditure, which is why wearables are excluded
EWMA
Exponentially weighted moving average — the smoother that extracts a weight trend from a noisy daily series (C6)
OLS
Ordinary least squares — plain linear regression; used for the slope of the trend (C6)
EKF
Extended Kalman filter — a recursive state estimator. Named only to record that it is excluded
DLW
Doubly labeled water — the reference method for measuring free-living expenditure. What the authoritative equations are built on and validated against
DXA
Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry — the body-composition reference method used alongside DLW in the validation study

Bodies, publications, corpus

DRI
Dietary Reference Intakes — the reference-value framework the PAL bands come from
NASEM
National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine — publisher of the 2023 energy DRI
IOM
Institute of Medicine — NASEM's former name; the source of the 2005-era bands still cited in the drafted spec
NIDDK
National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases — home of the dynamic body-weight model and the public Body Weight Planner
IOC
International Olympic Committee — publisher of the REDs consensus
AJCN
American Journal of Clinical Nutrition
IJO
International Journal of Obesity
CALERIE
The long-term calorie-restriction trial whose data validated the intake-balance method to within 40 kcal/d
DEC
This corpus's decision identifier, e.g. DEC-019. Readiness words attached to them — SHIPPED, COVERED, PROVISIONAL, BLOCKED, DEFERRED — are defined and owned by DEC_REGISTER.md; the gloss here is navigational only
MVP
Minimum viable product — here, the smallest set that individualizes honestly

Symbols

α
EWMA smoothing constant, between 0 and 1. Higher reacts faster and stays noisier; lower is steadier and lags. Open parameter (C6)
Δ
Change in — ΔES is change in energy stores, Δt the elapsed time
∂ES/∂mass
Partial derivative — kcal per kg of body-mass change; the coefficient a dynamic model supplies (C8)
Tₜ · Mₜ
Trend value and measured scale mass on day t (C6)
M_low · M_high
The low and high edges of the maintenance band (C5)
⇔
If and only if
∧
Logical AND — every condition must hold
≪
Much less than
±
Plus or minus — denotes a band, not a tolerance

Terms used in a specific sense here

Prior
The opening estimate before any of this person's own data exists — the profile-derived figure. Borrowed from Bayesian usage: a starting position that evidence is allowed to move
Band
A range carried all the way through, rather than a point. Collapsing one to its midpoint discards the method's own uncertainty — the app has already done that twice
Hard switchover
Replacing the prior with the observed estimate outright once the gate passes, rather than blending the two. Chosen because it needs no weighting function, which nobody is authorized to invent
Back-calculation
Inferring expenditure from what actually happened — intake plus observed weight change — instead of predicting it from a formula (C8)
Noise floor
The day-to-day scale variation from water, sodium, glycogen and gut content. A trend must clear it before it can be called real
Adaptive thermogenesis
The drop in expenditure after weight loss beyond what the smaller body alone explains. The reason a fixed target drifts out of calibration, and the argument for re-estimating rather than adding a correction term
Plausibility check
A one-question sanity test (C4) that can flag an estimate as reading high. It cannot correct one
