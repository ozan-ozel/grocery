# Topic Overlaps

Areas where two or more books cover substantially similar material, per `BOOK_TOPIC_COVERAGE.md`. For each: which books overlap, what kind of overlap it is (foundational / applied / clinical / research-oriented), and whether it reads as useful reinforcement or unnecessary duplication *at the TOC level*. No decision is made here about removing any book — that is explicitly out of scope for this phase.

---

## 1. Energy Metabolism (MET-03, MET-05, MET-07)

**Books:** HM4 (primary, foundational) and Bender3 (foundational, introductory level); AS3/ACSM/SN4 touch it only as applied context within their energy chapters.
**Nature:** Foundational/mechanistic. HM4 and Bender3 both build metabolism up from chemistry → enzymes → ATP → integration, but HM4 goes four TOC levels deep with a regulation-first frame while Bender3 stays at chapter/section level with a teach-from-scratch frame.
**Assessment:** Likely **useful reinforcement, not duplication** — the two books appear to target different entry points (Bender3 as an on-ramp, HM4 as the deeper treatment) rather than repeating the same altitude of content. A curriculum could sequence Bender3 → HM4, or use HM4 alone with Bender3 as a remedial/bridging resource. This is confirmed only at the TOC-structure level; whether the actual prose duplicates or complements can't be verified from a TOC alone (see `APPARENT_CURRICULUM_GAPS.md`'s caveats).

## 2. Carbohydrate Metabolism and Carbohydrate/Exercise (CHO-03, CHO-05)

**Books:** HM4 (mechanistic), Bender3 (mechanistic, lighter), AS3/ACSM/SN4/KM16 (applied, exercise-specific).
**Nature:** Splits cleanly into foundational (HM4/Bender3: pathways of glucose metabolism, fed/fasted regulation) vs. applied (AS3/ACSM/SN4/KM16: carbohydrate loading, intake during exercise, recovery, "train low compete high").
**Assessment:** **Useful reinforcement across the foundational/applied boundary**, but genuine **duplication risk within the applied cluster** — SN4's Chapter 6 and ACSM's Chapter 2 in particular cover nearly identical ground (recommendations, intake timing before/during/after exercise) at what appears from the TOC to be similar depth. KM16's Chapter 23 treatment is a third, clinically-flavored pass over the same applied ground.

## 3. Protein Metabolism and Protein Requirements (PRO-03, PRO-04, PRO-06)

**Books:** HM4, Bender3 (foundational/mechanistic); ACSM, SN4, KM16 Ch.23 (applied/exercise).
**Nature:** Same foundational/applied split as carbohydrate. Bender3's Chapter 9 is the deepest single-book treatment of protein metabolism mechanism (nitrogen balance, protein synthesis, amino acid metabolism) in the set.
**Assessment:** Foundational vs. applied split reads as reinforcement. Within the applied cluster, SN4 Ch.8 and ACSM Ch.3 both address protein requirements for exercise and could well be redundant with each other — same caveat as carbohydrate above.

## 4. Lipid Metabolism, Lipoproteins and Atherosclerosis (LIP-03, LIP-04, MET-09, CLIN-10)

**Books:** HM4 (Chapter 10, entirely devoted to this), KM16 (Chapter 33, clinical CVD framing), Bender3 (lighter mechanistic touch), SN4 (fat-as-fuel, exercise-specific).
**Nature:** A three-way split — HM4 is the deep biochemical/regulatory treatment, KM16 is the clinical/disease-management treatment of the *same underlying biology* (atherosclerosis, dyslipidemia, cardiovascular risk), and SN4 is the exercise-substrate-use treatment.
**Assessment:** This is the clearest example in the whole set of **complementary, non-duplicative overlap across the domain boundary** — three books look at the same biological system (lipoprotein transport and cholesterol metabolism) from three genuinely different purposes (mechanism, clinical management, exercise performance). A curriculum could deliberately sequence these together as one integrated cardiovascular-lipid module rather than three disconnected units.

## 5. Diabetes Mellitus (MET-10, CLIN-07)

**Books:** HM4 (Chapter 12, regulatory/mechanistic), Bender3 (§10.7, brief), KM16 (Chapter 30, the largest single disease chapter in the set), SN4/AS3/ACSM (not chaptered, though blood-glucose regulation is touched within exercise-metabolism content).
**Nature:** Foundational (HM4/Bender3) vs. clinical (KM16), with KM16's chapter being dramatically larger and covering type 1/2/gestational diabetes, screening, full nutrition-therapy protocols, medications, and complications in a way the other two do not attempt.
**Assessment:** **Complementary rather than duplicative** — HM4/Bender3 supply the "why," KM16 supplies the "what to do about it." No meaningful redundancy visible from the TOCs.

## 6. Hydration and Fluid/Electrolyte Balance (FLU-01–05)

**Books:** AS3 (Chapter 3, full chapter), ACSM (Chapter 7, full chapter), SN4 (Chapter 9, full chapter), KM16 (Chapter 3, full chapter — clinical framing; Chapter 23, exercise-specific subsection).
**Nature:** Three sport books cover essentially the same applied hydration-and-exercise content (thermoregulation, dehydration effects on performance, fluid replacement, electrolyte losses) as full standalone chapters. KM16's Chapter 3 is a different animal — general clinical fluid/electrolyte/acid-base physiology (water balance, calcium/sodium/magnesium/phosphorus/potassium physiology, acid-base disorders), not exercise-specific.
**Assessment:** The **AS3/ACSM/SN4 three-way overlap is the single strongest candidate for redundancy in the entire sport-nutrition portion of the set** — all three name a dedicated hydration/fluid chapter, and the TOC-level topic breakdown (thermoregulation, dehydration, fluid replacement, electrolyte losses) is close to identical across all three. KM16's chapter is complementary (general physiology/acid-base vs. exercise application) rather than duplicative.

## 7. Energy Balance and Body Composition (BODY-01–07)

**Books:** HM4 (Chapter 11, mechanistic), Bender3 (Chapter 6, obesity-focused), KM16 (Chapter 21, the largest weight-management treatment in the set), SN4 (two full chapters — Body Composition and Weight Management), AS3 (Chapter 10), ACSM (Chapter 8), NRM (Chapter 11 and 12, methods only).
**Nature:** This is the **most heavily overlapping topic cluster in the entire seven-book set** — six of the seven books have at least chapter-section-level content here, spanning foundational (HM4), clinical (KM16), sport-applied (AS3/ACSM/SN4), and methodological (NRM).
**Assessment:** A mix. NRM's methods content and HM4's mechanistic content are complementary to everyone else's applied content — genuine reinforcement. But among the three sport books (AS3 Ch.10, ACSM Ch.8, SN4 Ch.14–15), and separately between KM16 Ch.21 and Bender3 Ch.6, there is real topic-title-level duplication (obesity definitions, weight-loss dietary approaches, physical activity for weight management) that a curriculum designer should look at closely before deciding how many independent "weight management" units to build.

## 8. Sports Supplements and Ergogenic Aids (SPORT-05, SPECIAL-02)

**Books:** SN4 (Chapter 11, by far the most granular — ~35 named compounds), AS3 (Chapter 4), ACSM (Chapter 13), KM16 (Chapter 23 subsection, sport-specific; Chapter 11, general/non-sport supplements).
**Nature:** Applied, performance-oriented (SN4/AS3/ACSM/KM16-Ch23) vs. general clinical supplement regulation (KM16-Ch11, which is about supplement quality/regulation/safety in the general population, not sport performance).
**Assessment:** SN4's chapter is dramatically more exhaustive than AS3's or ACSM's at the level of individually named substances — this reads less like three equivalent treatments and more like **one comprehensive source (SN4) plus two lighter-touch treatments (AS3/ACSM) that likely summarize a subset of the same ground**. Whether that's useful reinforcement (repetition aids retention) or unnecessary duplication depends on curriculum design choices outside this phase's scope.

## 9. Nutrition Assessment (ASSESS-01–06)

**Books:** KM16 (three full chapters — the most complete assessment methodology in the set), NRM (two chapters, but framed as *research* methodology rather than clinical practice).
**Nature:** Applied-clinical (KM16) vs. research-methodological (NRM) — both address "how do you measure someone's diet/nutritional status," but for different purposes (patient care vs. study validity).
**Assessment:** **Complementary, not duplicative** — this is analogous to pattern #5 (diabetes): different purposes applied to the same underlying measurement problem. A curriculum could productively teach dietary-assessment *methods* once (drawing on both KM16 and NRM) and then branch into clinical-application vs. research-application tracks.

## 10. Dietary Recommendations / Reference Values (DRV-01–05, NUT-03)

**Books:** ACSM (a complete seven-appendix DRI reference set, athlete-oriented), KM16 (two DRI appendices plus ~20 individual-nutrient fact sheets, clinical/general-population-oriented), Bender3 (§11.1, methodology), AS3 (a single combined DRI appendix).
**Nature:** Reference-table material rather than narrative content — genuinely useful but mechanically repetitive by nature (RDA/AI/UL tables look similar no matter which book prints them).
**Assessment:** This is **duplication that is probably fine to keep as duplication** — reference tables are meant to be looked up, not read sequentially, so having the same DRI numbers in two or three appendices isn't pedagogically wasteful the way overlapping narrative chapters might be. Worth flagging for Phase 2 mainly so a single canonical reference-table set can be chosen if the curriculum wants one, rather than because the redundancy itself is a problem.

## 11. GI Physiology, Digestion and Absorption (GI-01, GI-02, CHO-02, LIP-02, PRO-02)

**Books:** HM4 (Chapter 4, full chapter), Bender3 (Chapter 4, full chapter), KM16 (Chapter 1, full chapter — but framed around "Intake" more broadly), SN4 (Chapter 5), AS3 (Chapter 5, exercise-specific), ACSM (Appendix K).
**Nature:** Foundational (HM4/Bender3) vs. clinical-intake framing (KM16) vs. exercise-specific (AS3/SN4).
**Assessment:** Similar pattern to lipid metabolism (#4) — genuinely complementary across purposes rather than duplicative, though HM4 and Bender3's chapter-4 treatments of digestion mechanics likely do overlap each other directly (both are foundational textbooks covering the same digestive physiology at a similar level).
