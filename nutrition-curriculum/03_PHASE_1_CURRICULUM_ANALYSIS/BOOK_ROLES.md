# Book Roles and Specialization

This maps what each book is *for* within the seven-book set, based only on TOC structure and emphasis. It does not rank the books — see Task 6's instruction — it maps specialization so Phase 2 can decide how to sequence and combine them.

---

## AS3 — Advanced Sports Nutrition, 3rd Edition (Benardot)

- **Primary domain:** SPORT (applied sports nutrition), organized by athlete type rather than by nutrient.
- **Secondary domains:** FLU (a full chapter on fluids/electrolytes), BODY-07 (body composition and weight), a short but present GI-04 (athlete GI function).
- **Strongest educational use:** Its four "strategy" chapters (Parts III–IV: Travel, Altitude, Gender and Age, Body Composition/Weight, then The Power Athlete / The Endurance Athlete / The Power and Endurance Athlete) are structured around *practical application to an athlete profile*, each with embedded sample eating plans — this is a distinctly applied/case-based angle none of the other six books use as their organizing principle.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Substantial topical overlap with ACSM and SN4 at the chapter-title level (Energy Nutrients / Vitamins and Minerals / Fluids and Electrolytes / Ergogenic Aids map closely to both). Its Part-level structure (only book in the set to use "Parts") is otherwise unique.
- **Unique coverage:** The by-athlete-type chapter structure (Power / Endurance / Power+Endurance) with sample eating plans has no direct counterpart — ACSM covers the same three categories in a *single* chapter (Ch. 11) rather than three, and SN4 doesn't organize by athlete archetype at all.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No chapter-level treatment of research methods, clinical/disease-state nutrition, or life-course nutrition beyond a single "Gender and Age" chapter. This is the oldest and least granular of the three sport-nutrition books in the set (no section-level page numbers were even printed in its own Contents — see its TOC file's Verification notes).
- **Appropriate role:** A practical, athlete-type-organized application layer once foundational sport-nutrition content has been taught elsewhere.

## ACSM — ACSM's Nutrition for Exercise Science, 1st Edition (Benardot / ACSM)

- **Primary domain:** SPORT, but organized nutrient-by-nutrient (mirrors a general nutrition textbook's structure applied to athletes) rather than by athlete type.
- **Secondary domains:** DRV (the only book in the set carrying a *complete*, standalone seven-appendix DRI reference set — Uses of DRIs, plus RDA/AI and UL tables for elements, vitamins, and macronutrients/water separately), ASSESS (light touch, Appendix H).
- **Strongest educational use:** Its appendix structure (A–L) is the most systematically organized reference-table resource of any book in the set for macronutrient/vitamin/mineral DRIs specifically for athletes, plus a standalone "Digestion and Absorption" appendix (K) — useful as a reference companion regardless of which chapter sequence is taught.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Chapter-by-chapter, this book's 15-chapter, nutrient-then-topic structure is the closest single match to SN4's chapter sequence of any book pair in the set (Carbohydrates → Protein → Lipids → Vitamins → Minerals → Hydration → Weight/Body Composition → ... → Supplements → Diet Planning largely parallels SN4's own progression). This is the strongest same-domain overlap in the whole collection.
- **Unique coverage:** Chapter 9's combined framing ("Oxygen Transport and Utilization, Reducing Muscle Soreness, and Improving Muscle Recovery") is a distinct synthesis not matched exactly elsewhere. Appendix L's single embedded case study ("A Runner Having Difficulty Achieving Her Goal") is the only worked case study named in any of the seven TOCs.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No printed page numbers survive in this EPUB (see its TOC file), which will matter for citing this book to students by page. No research-methods or life-course content beyond one "Age and Sex" chapter.
- **Appropriate role:** Either a alternative or a companion to SN4 for the nutrient-organized core of a sport-nutrition sequence, with its DRI appendices doubling as the curriculum's athlete-specific reference-table resource.

## HM4 — Human Metabolism: A Regulatory Perspective, 4th Edition (Frayn & Evans)

- **Primary domain:** MET (biochemical/physiological metabolism), by a wide margin the deepest and most systematically regulatory-focused book in the set.
- **Secondary domains:** GI (digestion chapter), BODY (a full chapter on energy balance/obesity), CLIN (light but real touches — diabetes gets its own chapter, and Chapter 9's "pathological situations" section covers trauma/infection/cancer metabolism at a mechanistic level).
- **Strongest educational use:** This is the only book in the set with a genuine Part→Chapter→Section→Subsection hierarchy that goes four levels deep and stays mechanistic throughout (hormonal control, gene expression, organ-specific pathways) — it is the natural biochemistry backbone underneath every other book's more applied treatment of carbohydrate/lipid/protein metabolism.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Meaningful conceptual overlap with Bender3 (both are metabolism-first textbooks — see `TOPIC_OVERLAPS.md`), and with MET-10/CLIN-07 (diabetes) and MET-09/CLIN-10 (lipoprotein/atherosclerosis) relative to KM16's clinical framing of the same diseases.
- **Unique coverage:** Chapter 6 ("Communication Systems") is the only chapter-length treatment of endocrine/nervous-system signaling as a *metabolic regulation* topic (as opposed to KM16's disease-by-disease endocrine chapter, which is clinical rather than mechanistic). Its "Metabolic Diary" framing in Chapter 7 (walking through a day's fed/fasted transitions) is a distinctive integrative pedagogical device not used elsewhere.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No sport-specific, life-course-specific (beyond a pregnancy/lactation subsection), or research-methods content. No public-health content.
- **Appropriate role:** The mechanistic/biochemical foundation layer that the applied books (AS3, ACSM, SN4) and the clinical book (KM16) can each build on without re-deriving the underlying regulatory physiology.

## Bender3 — Introduction to Nutrition and Metabolism, 3rd Edition (Bender)

- **Primary domain:** MET, but taught bottom-up from basic biochemistry (chemistry of covalent bonds → enzymes → ATP → digestion → macronutrient metabolism → micronutrients) rather than HM4's regulation-first approach.
- **Secondary domains:** PRO (a full, dedicated chapter on protein–energy malnutrition — the only book in the set to treat marasmus/kwashiorkor/cachexia as chapter-length content), BODY (overweight/obesity as its own chapter), VIT (11 named vitamins each as an individually numbered section — the most granular vitamin-by-vitamin treatment of any book here).
- **Strongest educational use:** Its 11-chapter arc reads as a genuine *introductory course* in nutritional biochemistry — each chapter builds directly on the last (enzymes → ATP → digestion → energy metabolism → malnutrition → protein → integration → micronutrients). No other book in the set has this same designed-as-a-single-course linearity.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Chapters 1–3 (enzymes, ATP, metabolic pathways) meaningfully overlap HM4's mechanistic content, though pitched at an entry level rather than HM4's depth. Chapter 11 (micronutrients) parallels AS3/ACSM/SN4's vitamin-and-mineral chapters but from a pure-biochemistry rather than performance angle.
- **Unique coverage:** Chapter 7's title framing, "Diet and the diseases of affluence" (with sections on evidence linking diet to disease, free radicals/antioxidants, and protective non-nutrients), is a distinct epidemiological-nutrition topic not chaptered this way anywhere else in the set.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** Oldest book in the set (2002/2004); no sport-specific, clinical-disease-specific (beyond diabetes and PEM), research-methods, or life-course content.
- **Appropriate role:** An entry-level biochemistry/metabolism on-ramp, potentially positioned *before* HM4 for students without a strong prior biochemistry background, or as the primary metabolism text if HM4's depth is judged too advanced for the target audience.

## NRM — Nutrition Research Methodologies, 2015 (Lovegrove, Hodson, Sharma & Lanham-New)

- **Primary domain:** RESEARCH, exclusively — this is the only book in the set with no meaningful chapter-level presence in any other domain.
- **Secondary domains:** None at chapter level; light cross-domain touches only (Chapter 11's body-composition-assessment content brushes BODY/ASSESS, Chapter 9's gut-microbiome mention brushes GI).
- **Strongest educational use:** The single source in the whole collection for how nutrition science itself is *done* — study design (population and intervention), dietary-assessment methodology, biomarkers, statistics, omics/systems biology, animal/cellular models, and translation of research to policy. Every other book in the set presents nutrition *findings*; only this one teaches how those findings are produced and evaluated.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Essentially none at the chapter level. Its Chapter 4 (dietary-assessment methods) is the one place where meaningful conceptual overlap exists with KM16's Chapter 4 (dietary-history assessment) — but NRM treats it as a *research-methodology* question (validity, reproducibility, measurement error) while KM16 treats it as a *clinical* question (what to ask a patient).
- **Unique coverage:** Chapters 9 (biobanks), 13 (omics technologies), 16 (systems-biology data methods), 17 (stable isotopes), 18–19 (animal and cellular models), and 20 (translation of research) have no equivalent anywhere else in the set — these are entirely single-sourced.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No nutrient-specific, disease-specific, sport-specific, or life-course content at all — by design, this book assumes that content is taught elsewhere and focuses purely on methodology.
- **Appropriate role:** A dedicated "how we know what we know" module, most naturally positioned either as a standalone research-literacy unit or interleaved once students already have nutrient/metabolism grounding from MET/CHO/LIP/PRO/VIT/MIN, so the methods have concrete subject matter to apply to.

## SN4 — Sport Nutrition, 4th Edition (Jeukendrup & Gleeson)

- **Primary domain:** SPORT — the largest, most current (2025), and most granular of the three sport-nutrition books in the set (17 chapters, 683 pages, full page-level mapping throughout).
- **Secondary domains:** BODY (two full chapters — Body Composition and Weight Management, more than any other book), FLU (a full chapter), VIT/MIN (a combined chapter), SPECIAL-02/CLIN-20 overlap (Eating Disorders/Low Energy Availability as its own chapter, unlike AS3/ACSM which don't chapter this separately).
- **Strongest educational use:** Chapter 11 ("Nutrition Supplements") is by a wide margin the most exhaustive single-source treatment of ergogenic aids and supplements in the entire seven-book set — roughly 35 individually named compounds, each apparently given its own subsection, versus AS3/ACSM's single undifferentiated "Ergogenic Aids"/"Dietary Supplements" chapters. Chapter 17 ("Personalized Nutrition," including "Genetic Influences") is the only chapter-length treatment of precision/personalized sport nutrition in the set.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** The strongest overlap of any pair in the collection is SN4 vs. ACSM (see AS3's entry above and `TOPIC_OVERLAPS.md`) — both are nutrient-organized, 15–17-chapter sport-nutrition texts covering nearly the same sequence of topics. SN4 vs. AS3 overlaps at the topic-title level (energy, carbohydrate, fat, protein, hydration, vitamins/minerals, supplements, body composition) but AS3 organizes its second half by athlete type while SN4 stays nutrient-organized throughout.
- **Unique coverage:** Chapter 12 ("Nutrition and Training Adaptations," including signal-transduction pathways and effects on sleep/rehabilitation) and Chapter 13 ("Nutrition and Immune Function in Athletes") are each the most chapter-length, dedicated treatments of their subjects in the set.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No clinical/disease-state content beyond athlete-specific eating disorders; no research-methods content; no general (non-sport) life-course content.
- **Appropriate role:** The most likely candidate for the curriculum's *primary* sport-nutrition spine, given its recency, granularity, and full page-level verifiability — with AS3 and ACSM as supplementary/alternative sources for the same domain (see `TOPIC_OVERLAPS.md` for whether that overlap is reinforcing or redundant).

## KM16 — Krause and Mahan's Food and the Nutrition Care Process, 16th Edition (Raymond & Morrow)

- **Primary domain:** CLIN — by a very wide margin the largest and most singularly-focused book in the set. 45 chapters, ~1,214 pages, 50 appendices; roughly 27 of its 45 chapters map to a distinct CLIN disease-category topic that literally no other book in the set touches at chapter level.
- **Secondary domains:** LIFE (a full life-course arc — pregnancy/lactation, infancy, childhood, adolescence, transgender, adult, aging — all as individual chapters, unmatched by any other book), ASSESS (a genuinely complete assessment methodology across three chapters — dietary history, biochemical/physical/functional, and diagnosis/intervention), PUBHEALTH (its entire Chapter 8), FLU (its own dedicated chapter), NUT-03/DRV-05 (an enormous cluster of therapeutic-diet and single-nutrient reference appendices).
- **Strongest educational use:** This is the only book in the set that could plausibly stand alone as a comprehensive clinical/medical nutrition therapy reference — its 45-chapter structure essentially *is* a full disease-by-disease MNT curriculum, each chapter following the same Etiology → Pathophysiology → Medical Management → Medical Nutrition Therapy template.
- **Likely redundancy with other books:** Real but narrow overlap exists at a handful of specific points: diabetes (with HM4's Ch. 12 and Bender3's §10.7), cardiovascular disease/lipoproteins (with HM4's Ch. 10), critical-care/metabolic-stress physiology (with HM4's Ch. 8–9), and sport-adjacent chapters (Ch. 21 Weight Management, Ch. 22 Eating Disorders, Ch. 23 Exercise/Sports Performance) which cover ground SN4/AS3/ACSM also cover, from a clinical rather than performance angle.
- **Unique coverage:** The overwhelming majority of this book's content is unique in the set — every disease-specific chapter from GI disorders (Ch. 27–28) through genetic metabolic disorders (Ch. 44) and intellectual/developmental disabilities (Ch. 45) has no counterpart anywhere else in these seven TOCs. See `APPARENT_CURRICULUM_GAPS.md` for the implication of this single-sourcing.
- **Limitations (TOC-visible only):** No research-methodology content (that's NRM's exclusive domain) and comparatively thin mechanistic biochemistry (that depth lives in HM4/Bender3) — KM16 assumes that grounding is already in place and builds clinical application on top of it. Also: this file's own Verification notes flag that page numbers below chapter/appendix level are unavailable in this specific PDF export (a file-quality limitation, not a content limitation).
- **Appropriate role:** The clinical/medical-nutrition-therapy capstone of the curriculum — the domain that everything else (MET's biochemistry, CHO/LIP/PRO/VIT/MIN's nutrient science, ASSESS's methodology, LIFE's life-course grounding) ultimately feeds into for disease-specific application.

## Cross-Book Pattern Summary

| Pattern | Books involved |
|---|---|
| Three books share the SPORT domain with meaningfully different organizing principles (by-athlete-type vs. nutrient-by-nutrient vs. nutrient-by-nutrient-plus-precision) | AS3, ACSM, SN4 |
| Two books share the MET domain at different depths/pedagogical entry points (introductory vs. advanced/regulatory) | Bender3, HM4 |
| One book owns RESEARCH outright | NRM |
| One book owns CLIN outright | KM16 |
| LIFE-course content is entirely single-sourced | KM16 |
| DRV reference tables are duplicated in two different forms (athlete-specific vs. general/clinical) by two different books | ACSM, KM16 |
