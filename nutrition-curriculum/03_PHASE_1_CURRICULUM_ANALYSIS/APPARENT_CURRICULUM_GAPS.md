# Apparent Curriculum Gaps

**Important caveat, stated once here and implied throughout:** everything below is an *apparent* gap, visible only from what these seven Tables of Contents do or don't name as a heading. A TOC cannot prove a topic is absent from a book's actual text — a subject could be woven into a chapter's prose without earning its own TOC entry, especially in books like AS3 (which only prints Part/Chapter-level headings, no sections) or ACSM (whose EPUB has no page-level structure to inspect further). Nothing here should be read as "book X doesn't cover Y" — only as "book X's TOC doesn't name Y as a heading."

---

## 1. Subjects Absent from All Seven TOCs

None of the seven books' TOCs name any of the following as a heading, at any level:

- **Food science / culinary technique** — no book covers cooking methods, recipe development, or food preparation science as a subject (KM16's appendices on therapeutic diets tell you *what* to eat, not *how* to prepare it).
- **Health economics / nutrition policy costing** — KM16's PUBHEALTH content covers guidelines and programs but not their economics; NRM's Chapter 20 ("Translation of Nutrition Research") touches policy communication, not policy economics.
- **Global/international nutrition and food security in low-resource settings** — KM16's public-health chapter is explicitly U.S.-oriented (NHANES, USDA programs, Dietary Guidelines *for Americans*); no book in the set names a chapter on international or global-health nutrition, famine, or humanitarian nutrition.
- **Sustainability/environmental nutrition beyond one subsection** — KM16 Chapter 8 has a single subsection, "Healthy Food and Water Systems and Sustainability," which is the only place this appears anywhere in the set; not developed further by any other book.
- **Nutrition informatics / health technology** beyond a single KM16 subsection ("Electronic Health Records and Nutrition Informatics").
- **Ethics in nutrition research or clinical practice** as a named topic — KM16 mentions "Ethical Issues" once (within nutrition-support-in-long-term-care), and NRM's research-design chapters presumably touch consent/ethics implicitly (RCT design, biobanks) but no TOC entry names research ethics or clinical ethics explicitly.
- **Regulatory/legal frameworks for food and nutrition** beyond KM16's brief "Nutrition and Health Claims Regulation" and "Nonregulation of Nutrition Supplements" subsections.

## 2. Subjects Represented by Only One Specialized Source

These are real topics in the set, but every ounce of their coverage comes from a single book — if that book is dropped or de-emphasized, the topic disappears entirely from the curriculum:

- **All of RESEARCH (research methodology)** — 100% NRM. No other book has a single chapter on study design, dietary-assessment validity, biomarkers, statistics, omics methods, biobanks, or animal/cellular models.
- **The overwhelming majority of CLIN (clinical/disease-specific nutrition)** — roughly 27 of KM16's 45 chapters have zero counterpart elsewhere: food allergies, upper/lower GI disorders, hepatobiliary/pancreatic disease, thyroid/adrenal disorders, anemia, pulmonary disease, renal disease, cancer, infectious disease, HIV/AIDS, critical care, rheumatic/musculoskeletal disease, neurologic disorders, psychiatric/cognitive disorders, low-birth-weight infants, genetic metabolic disorders, and intellectual/developmental disabilities.
- **All of LIFE (life-course nutrition)** except for exercise-context mentions of age/sex — pregnancy/lactation, infancy, childhood, adolescence, adulthood, aging, and transgender nutrition care are all single-sourced to KM16.
- **Bone health and oral/dental health** — KM16 only (Chapters 24–25).
- **Nutrition counseling/behavioral-change theory** — KM16 only (Chapter 13).
- **Cultural competency in nutrition care** — KM16 only (part of Chapter 10).
- **The most granular sports-supplement compound-by-compound coverage** — SN4 only (Chapter 11's ~35 named substances); AS3/ACSM cover the *category* but not with SN4's individual-compound depth.
- **Precision/personalized sport nutrition (genetics-informed)** — SN4 only (Chapter 17).
- **Athlete immune function as a dedicated topic** — SN4 only (Chapter 13); ACSM touches recovery/oxygen-transport but doesn't name immune function specifically.
- **Sport-specific training-adaptation signaling (molecular exercise physiology)** — SN4 only (Chapter 12).

## 3. Weakly Represented Subjects (Present but Thin)

Topics that appear in more than one book's TOC but never rise above a minor subsection or single appendix anywhere:

- **Alcohol as a nutrition topic** — appears only as a subsection within larger chapters (SN4 Ch.1, KM16's pregnancy/CVD/bone-health/cancer chapters each mention alcohol briefly) and one appendix (KM16 App. 24). No book gives alcohol and nutrition its own chapter-level treatment despite it recurring across at least six different clinical/applied contexts.
- **Caffeine** — similarly scattered (SN4 supplements chapter, KM16 heart-failure/bone-health chapters, KM16 App. 25) without ever being the primary subject of a chapter.
- **Food security / food insecurity** at the individual-patient level — appears as a subsection within KM16's transgender-nutrition and community-nutrition chapters, never developed as its own topic.
- **COVID-19 and nutrition** — appears repeatedly across at least six different KM16 chapters (obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular, infectious disease, rheumatic disease) as a recurring but always-secondary subsection, reflecting the book's edition timing rather than a designed curriculum topic. Worth flagging because it's simultaneously "present" (mentioned six times) and "absent" (never its own topic) — an artifact of TOC-only analysis this gap list is specifically designed to surface.
- **Nutrigenomics/epigenetics** — real content exists in three different books (NRM Ch.14–15 as research methods, KM16 Ch.6 as clinical application) but no book bridges the two into a single coherent nutrigenomics arc; see `MASTER_TOPIC_UNIVERSE.md`'s SPECIAL-05 entry, which flags this exact seam.
- **Doping / performance-enhancing drugs in sport** — a real subsection (KM16 Ch.23 "Performance Enhancement Substances and Drugs: Doping In Sport") but not chaptered anywhere, despite being a distinct regulatory/ethical topic adjacent to (but different from) legal ergogenic aids/supplements.

## 4. Subjects That Likely Depend on External/Current Literature

Topics where even the newest book in the set (SN4, copyright 2025; KM16, copyright 2023) may already be behind fast-moving science, based purely on subject matter that is known (from these TOCs' own framing) to be an active research area:

- **GLP-1 and newer pharmacologic weight-management agents** — KM16's obesity chapter names "Pharmaceutical Management" and its diabetes chapter names "Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists," but given this is one of the fastest-moving areas in clinical nutrition/endocrinology right now, curriculum designers should treat this subsection as a placeholder needing a currency check rather than a finished topic — this is a judgment call for Phase 2/3, not something resolvable from a TOC.
- **Continuous glucose monitoring and diabetes technology** — named in KM16 (Ch.30) but likely to be one of the fastest-evolving subsections in the whole set.
- **Gut microbiome science** — appears in five different books' TOCs (NRM, SN4, KM16 ×3) but always as a subsection, never a chapter, despite being one of the most active research areas nutrition science touches; this pattern (present everywhere, developed nowhere) is worth flagging for a currency/depth review in Phase 2.
- **Personalized/precision nutrition broadly** — SN4's Chapter 17 is dated 2025 and already frames this as an emerging area; any curriculum built on this chapter should expect it to need refreshing faster than the rest of the set.

## 5. Structural Observations (Not Gaps, But Relevant to Gap Analysis)

- **AS3's TOC has no section-level entries at all** (see its own Verification notes — the printed Contents lists only Part/Chapter titles). This means AS3's *apparent* thinness relative to ACSM/SN4 in `BOOK_TOPIC_COVERAGE.md` may partly be a TOC-granularity artifact rather than a true content difference — AS3 could contain just as much section-level detail as SN4 without it being visible from the TOC alone.
- **ACSM's EPUB carries no page numbers**, which doesn't affect topic coverage but will matter when Phase 2/3 needs to cite specific pages.
- **KM16's PDF has broken page destinations below chapter/appendix level** — all of its rich subsection detail (the deepest and most extensive in the set) is present and was captured, but without page numbers, which will matter for citation but not for topic-mapping purposes.

## 6. Summary — What This Means for Phase 2

If the eventual curriculum wants complete coverage in RESEARCH, LIFE, or the bulk of CLIN, there is currently **no redundancy to fall back on** — those domains stand or fall entirely on NRM and KM16 respectively. Any future decision to drop, replace, or supplement either book should account for this single-source dependency explicitly. This document does not recommend acquiring additional books or making that decision — it only surfaces where the dependency exists.
