# Research Architecture

Possible placements for NRM's 15 RESEARCH topics within the curriculum. Per `PHASE_1_AMBIGUITY_AUDIT.md` item 9g, the fact that RESEARCH is 100% single-sourced to NRM is already **resolved** (confirmed, quantified, not in question). Whether that single-source dependency is *acceptable* as the foundation for this domain is an explicit **human decision that this file does not resolve** — it is restated, not silently closed, in the final section below and carried forward to `PHASE_2_HUMAN_REVIEW.md`.

---

## Structural Starting Point

Per `LEARNING_DEPENDENCY_GRAPH.md` Chain 7, RESEARCH is a near-island: an internally coherent 15-topic chain (RESEARCH-01 through RESEARCH-15, itself well-sequenced — see `TOPIC_PREREQUISITES.md`) with exactly two points of contact to the rest of the graph:
- A **soft** (HELPFUL) link to ASSESS-01 (dietary-assessment methodology overlap, `TOPIC_OVERLAPS.md` #9 — complementary, not duplicative).
- A **REQUIRED** dependency *from* MET (basic biochemistry/gene expression) *into* RESEARCH-11 (omics technologies) — meaning RESEARCH is not entirely prerequisite-free; it needs some MET foundation before its own omics/epigenetics topics (RESEARCH-11/12) make sense.

This structural isolation is what makes multiple placement options genuinely viable — RESEARCH doesn't have to sit in any one specific place in the sequence the way, say, CLIN-01 does (which has 26 topics depending on it).

---

## Four Placement Options

### A. Independent Parallel Track

RESEARCH runs alongside the nutrient/metabolism/applied core as its own strand throughout the curriculum — a student takes "core nutrition content" and "research methods" concurrently, in separate but simultaneous sequences, meeting only where the graph already shows a natural touch point (RESEARCH-11 needs MET; RESEARCH-04 complements ASSESS-01).

**Fit:** Strong structural fit, since RESEARCH really is separable. Lowest risk of RESEARCH being either neglected (buried at the end) or forced into an artificial position it doesn't need.
**Cost:** Requires a genuinely parallel-track curriculum design (two concurrent strands), which is more complex to schedule/deliver than a single linear sequence — an implementation question, not resolved here.

### B. Early Introduction + Continuous Reinforcement

RESEARCH-01 (Nature and Purpose of Nutrition Research) is introduced early, close to NUT-01, as a "how do we know what we know" framing device for the whole curriculum — then RESEARCH-02 through RESEARCH-15 are distributed in small doses throughout the rest of the sequence, each surfacing exactly when its subject matter becomes relevant (e.g., RESEARCH-06 Biomarkers introduced alongside ASSESS-02 Biochemical Assessment; RESEARCH-11/12 Omics introduced alongside MET-04's gene-expression content).

**Fit:** Good — leverages the two real touch points identified above, and NRM's own Chapter 1 framing ("Nature, Purpose and Implications of Research in Nutrition," per `MASTER_TOPIC_UNIVERSE.md`'s RESEARCH-01 provenance) is explicitly written as this kind of orienting-frame content, suggesting the source book itself supports an early-introduction posture for at least that one topic.
**Cost:** The remaining 14 RESEARCH topics don't have as many natural touch points to hang reinforcement moments on — RESEARCH-09 (Biobanks), RESEARCH-13 (Stable Isotopes), RESEARCH-14 (Animal/Cellular Models) in particular have **zero** cross-domain edges in `TOPIC_PREREQUISITES.md`, so "continuous reinforcement" for those specific topics would require inventing touch points not actually supported by the topic universe — a risk this architecture should be aware of rather than forcing.

### C. Post-Foundation Research Track

RESEARCH is taught as a single block, but positioned *after* the shared foundation (Layer 1 in `CLINICAL_NUTRITION_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s terms — MET/CHO/LIP/PRO/VIT/MIN/GI/BODY) is already in place, rather than at the very start of the curriculum or fully interleaved throughout.

**Fit:** Directly satisfies RESEARCH-11's REQUIRED dependency on MET without needing partial/early MET content specifically pulled forward (as Option B would). Also matches how NRM itself is positioned in `BOOK_ROLES.md`'s stated recommendation: *"most naturally positioned...once students already have nutrient/metabolism grounding...so the methods have concrete subject matter to apply to."*
**Cost:** Loses the "how do we know what we know" framing value that Option B's early introduction of RESEARCH-01 specifically offers — a tradeoff between epistemic framing (early) and applied relevance (later), not a strict win for either.

### D. Hybrid Model

RESEARCH-01 only is pulled early (as an orienting frame, per Option B's rationale), the bulk of RESEARCH-02 through RESEARCH-10 and RESEARCH-15 form a coherent post-foundation block (as in Option C, once MET/ASSESS content exists to connect to), and RESEARCH-11/12/13/14 (the most specialized, SPECIALIZED-tier topics per `TOPIC_LEARNING_LEVELS.md`: omics, epigenetics, stable isotopes, animal/cellular models) are positioned as an optional advanced module *after* the main RESEARCH block, available to students continuing toward a research-focused track rather than required of everyone.

**Fit:** Directly reflects the actual heterogeneity within RESEARCH's own 15 topics — RESEARCH-01 through RESEARCH-10 (plus 15) are already classified INTERMEDIATE/ADVANCED (general research literacy), while RESEARCH-09/11/12/13/14 are SPECIALIZED (per `TOPIC_LEARNING_LEVELS.md`) — this option is the only one that treats RESEARCH's internal FOUNDATION→SPECIALIZED gradient (FOUNDATION: 1 topic; INTERMEDIATE: 4; ADVANCED: 5; SPECIALIZED: 5) as architecturally meaningful rather than treating the domain as a single undifferentiated block.
**Cost:** Most complex of the four to implement — effectively combines elements of B, C, and `CANDIDATE_EXCLUSIONS.md`-style elective-tiering into one design.

## Comparison at a Glance

| Dimension | A. Parallel Track | B. Early + Reinforcement | C. Post-Foundation Block | D. Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Respects RESEARCH-11's MET dependency | Yes (touch point preserved) | Requires care (MET not fully taught yet at "early" point) | Yes, cleanly | Yes, cleanly |
| Preserves NRM's own "orienting frame" framing (RESEARCH-01) | Only if scheduled early in the parallel track | Yes, directly | No | Yes, for RESEARCH-01 specifically |
| Handles RESEARCH's internal FOUNDATION→SPECIALIZED spread | Not explicitly | Not explicitly | Not explicitly | Yes, explicitly |
| Implementation complexity | High (true concurrency) | Moderate | Low | Highest |
| Risk of forcing unsupported touch points | Low | Moderate (for the 3 zero-edge topics) | Low | Low |

## Explicitly Not Resolved Here

**The Phase 1 human decision — is 100%-NRM single-source dependency acceptable as the foundation for the entire RESEARCH domain? — is restated, not resolved, by any of the four options above.** All four options describe *where* RESEARCH sits in a sequence; none of them addresses *whether* building 15 required or elective topics on one book's TOC alone is an acceptable curriculum foundation, or whether that should prompt seeking supplementary research-methods material before Phase 3 commits to teaching content. That decision belongs to `PHASE_2_HUMAN_REVIEW.md` (item 5) and is not silently closed by choosing any placement option here — a curriculum could adopt Option D's careful internal tiering and still be entirely dependent on one 2015 textbook for all of it.
