# Topic Learning Levels

Every Level-1 topic in `MASTER_TOPIC_UNIVERSE.md` classified by **educational dependency and conceptual complexity** — not by importance, not by how many books cover it, and not by curriculum priority. A topic can be FOUNDATION and still be optional in the final curriculum (see `CANDIDATE_EXCLUSIONS.md`); a topic can be SPECIALIZED and still be essential for a given student population.

- **FOUNDATION** — assumes no prior nutrition/metabolism knowledge; introduces core concepts and vocabulary.
- **INTERMEDIATE** — builds directly on FOUNDATION topics; still core/general knowledge expected of any nutrition student.
- **ADVANCED** — requires FOUNDATION + INTERMEDIATE groundwork; involves integration across topics or applied clinical/performance reasoning.
- **SPECIALIZED** — narrow scope, single-source in this set, or intended for a specific sub-audience (a particular clinical subspecialty, a research-methods specialist, an elite-sport practitioner) rather than every student.

Subtopics (Level-2 IDs) are not separately classified — each inherits its parent topic's level unless explicitly noted otherwise below.

## Notes on Method

- Classification followed `TOPIC_PREREQUISITES.md`'s dependency chains wherever a chain existed — a topic sitting at the *end* of a long REQUIRED chain (e.g., MET-07, MET-08) was classified ADVANCED regardless of how "basic" its subject sounds in isolation, because genuine comprehension depends on everything upstream.
- Several topics were classified SPECIALIZED **not because they are unimportant**, but because they are single-sourced, narrow-audience, or address a specific practice niche (rare disease chapters in CLIN, methodological specialties in RESEARCH, transgender/gender-diverse care, personalized/genomic nutrition). This matches the Task 2 instruction that this is not a ranking.
- CLIN's 27 topics split roughly 15 ADVANCED / 11 SPECIALIZED / 1 INTERMEDIATE (CLIN-26) — reflecting that most disease-specific chapters assume the same intermediate clinical grounding (ADVANCED tier) while a subset addresses genuinely narrow practice areas (SPECIALIZED tier: HIV/AIDS, critical care, rheumatic disease, neurologic disorders, psychiatric disorders, LBW infants, genetic metabolic disorders, IDD, oral health, transgender care).
- No topic was classified FOUNDATION solely because it "sounds simple" — DRV's reference-table topics, for instance, are INTERMEDIATE rather than FOUNDATION because interpreting an RDA/UL table meaningfully requires the NUT-02/MET grounding to already be in place.
